# Recognize, Verify, Report

Suspicious messages are not always obvious. They can look like normal requests from a coworker, vendor, or service your organization uses.

You are not expected to investigate every message. The goal is to notice when a request needs verification and know how to report it.

## 1. Pause

Take a closer look when a message asks you to:

- Sign in because an account supposedly has a problem.
- Change payment or bank details.
- Open an unexpected shared document or attachment.
- Share a password, verification code, or sensitive information.
- Act urgently or bypass a normal approval process.

A familiar name is not proof that a message is legitimate. An attacker may impersonate a person or use an account that has been compromised.

## 2. Verify

Use a trusted channel that is separate from the suspicious request.

- **Account alert:** Open the service from your bookmark or by typing its known address. Do not use the message's link.
- **Payment change:** Call the vendor using a number from `[KNOWN VENDOR PHONE DIRECTORY]`. Do not use a new number supplied in the suspicious request.
- **Shared document:** Ask the sender through an established channel whether they intended to share it.

If you are still unsure, report the message and ask for help.

## 3. Report

Use `[REPORTING METHOD]` or contact `[SECURITY CONTACT]`.

If you clicked a link, entered information, approved an unexpected sign-in, opened an attachment, or sent money, contact `[URGENT CONTACT]` immediately.

Tell IT what happened. Reporting quickly—even after a mistake—gives the organization more options to respond.

## Keep in mind

- Do not share passwords or one-time verification codes.
- Treat an unexpected multifactor authentication prompt as suspicious. Deny it and contact IT.
- Do not reply to a suspicious message or use its contact details to verify it.
- Follow the organization's normal process for approving payment changes.
- You do not need to prove that a message is malicious before reporting it.

**When in doubt: pause, verify, and report.**
